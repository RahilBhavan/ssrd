When scam sites return,
does the software match?

Certificate Transparency monitor matching newly certified domains against fingerprints of known crypto-investment scam kits. A kit match means shared site software, never operator attribution.

SSRD is the Scam-Site Respawn Detector. It compares newly certified domains with a library of shared crypto-investment site software. A match is an investigative lead. It does not identify an operator or prove that a site is a scam.

Read this page in order: see what entered the pipeline, inspect what has been measured, then try a synthetic review. Counts describe the monitor's coverage; human review is needed to estimate alert precision.

    How to read the numbers

    CT names & candidates
    Certificate Transparency (CT) records certificate issuance, not website content. Names are filtered into candidates. The headline names count uses the metrics window; candidates are database totals, so these are not a single conversion cohort. Pass rate divides recorded filter passes by recorded names in that window.
    Visits & outcomes
    A visit is a queued passive retrieval, not a unique site or a human visit. Content means a usable page, not a confirmed scam. Blank, parked, blocked and error outcomes describe retrieval results; expired jobs missed their due window. Outcome shares use the sum of recorded outcome counts.
    Kits, seeds & alerts
    Seeds are reference pages from source lists, not adjudications. A kit groups pages sharing software features. Single-site references have no multi-domain cluster. Active alerts satisfy the current rule; cumulative alerts include historical decisions. KCDA and CBR name source groups, not operator identities. In the library, p means pages and d means domains.
    Signals & scores
    A is page content, B is shared assets, C is page features and D is infrastructure. Signal counts can overlap: one alert may contribute to several rows. A match score combines configured evidence weights; it is not a probability of wrongdoing.
    Precision & backtest
    Precision is the scam-kit share of human-reviewed alerts. A reviewed subset can be biased; a frozen random sample is needed for a representative estimate. The 95% interval describes sampling uncertainty, not unobserved bias. Backtest catch rate is held-out reference pages matched to the correct kit; it is not live precision.
    Latency & other checks
    Median is the middle CT-to-alert delay; p90 is the delay containing 90% of included alerts. Later kit rebuilds are counted separately. Recall is the fraction of known positives selected. CBR forward checks identify later list appearances. Cohen's κ measures agreement beyond chance between two human reviewers; absent measurements remain pending.
